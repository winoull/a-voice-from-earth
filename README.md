1. Abstract

A Voice from Earth is an independent scientific and technological initiative designed to create and transmit a digital radio message containing an authentic human voice from planet Earth into deep space.

The project aims to preserve a short personal voice recording belonging to Mohamed Ouchane, a human being from Earth, and transform it into a structured digital message suitable for radio transmission beyond our planet.

The message will contain three languages:

- Arabic
- Moroccan Amazigh (Tachelhit)
- English

In addition to the original voice recording, the message will include basic astronomical information describing Earth's location within the Solar System and the Milky Way.

The project will investigate the technical feasibility of transmitting this message through an appropriate radio astronomy or deep-space communication facility.

Its primary purpose is to create a small but meaningful digital record of human existence and curiosity, directed toward the unknown universe.

This project does not assume the existence of extraterrestrial civilizations or guarantee that another intelligence will receive the message.

It is an attempt to leave a recognizable human signature in the universe.

2. Project Vision

Humanity has been transmitting radio signals into space for more than a century.

However, this project has a different personal objective.

Rather than transmitting a general scientific broadcast, A Voice from Earth seeks to preserve one identifiable human voice.

The founder wishes to send a message containing:

- A personal name.
- An authentic human voice.
- Multiple human languages.
- A description of the planet of origin.
- A scientifically understandable digital encoding format.

The message is intended to represent one individual from Earth while symbolically representing humanity's curiosity about the universe.

Core statement:

"Somewhere on Earth, a human being decided to send his voice into the unknown universe."

5. Voice Message Design

The original voice message will have a total target duration of approximately 30 seconds.

The recording will be divided into three language segments.

5.1 Arabic Segment

Proposed message:

"أنا محمد أشن، إنسان من كوكب الأرض. أرسل صوتي عبر هذا الكون الواسع، كعلامة صغيرة على وجودنا ورغبتنا في التواصل."

5.2 Moroccan Amazigh Segment

The second segment will be recorded in Moroccan Tachelhit.

The final text will be reviewed by a competent Tachelhit speaker before recording.

The project will preserve the original language and pronunciation rather than replacing it with a synthetic voice.

5.3 English Segment

Proposed message:

"My name is Mohamed Ouchane. I come from planet Earth. This is my voice, a message of peace and curiosity, sent into the unknown universe. Wherever you are, greetings from Earth."

5.4 Recording Requirements

Recommended recording specifications:

- Format: WAV.
- Sample rate: 48 kHz.
- Bit depth: 24-bit.
- Channels: Mono.
- Recording environment: Quiet indoor studio.
- Processing: Noise reduction only when necessary.
- Original recording: Preserved without destructive processing.

The recording should be made by the project founder.

No artificial voice should replace the original human recording.

6. Digital Signal Architecture

The message will be prepared as a digital data package.

The proposed processing pipeline is:

1. Record the original voice.
2. Convert the recording into a standardized digital audio format.
3. Create a metadata document.
4. Generate a cryptographic SHA-256 checksum.
5. Package the audio and metadata.
6. Apply error-correction coding.
7. Apply a documented modulation scheme.
8. Prepare the radio transmission specification.

6.1 Audio Encoding

The original audio will be archived in uncompressed WAV format.

A compressed version may be created to reduce the amount of transmitted data.

Both versions should be preserved.

6.2 Binary Representation

The digital information will ultimately be represented as binary data.

A receiver must be able to reconstruct the original information using the published encoding specification.

6.3 Error Correction

Interstellar transmission may involve weak signals and noise.

The project will investigate suitable forward error correction methods, including:

- Reed-Solomon coding.
- Convolutional coding.
- LDPC coding.
- Synchronization sequences.

The final method will depend on the transmission facility and receiver requirements.

6.4 Data Integrity

SHA-256 will be used to verify the integrity of the original audio and final data package.

The checksum will help determine whether a reconstructed recording matches the original digital file.

A checksum does not itself provide encryption or authentication.

7. Astronomical Identification

A central component of the project is providing information about the origin of the message.

The message should not simply state that it originated from Earth.

It should provide a scientifically meaningful description of our astronomical location.

7.1 Earth

- Planet: Earth.
- Star: Sun.
- Planetary system: Solar System.
- Galaxy: Milky Way.
- Galactic structure: Orion Spur.

7.2 Astronomical Reference Systems

The project will investigate the use of:

- International Celestial Reference Frame (ICRF).
- Solar System barycentric coordinates.
- Standard astronomical time references.
- Pulsar-based navigation references.

7.3 Pulsar-Based Location Map

Pulsars are rapidly rotating neutron stars that produce highly regular radio pulses.

Their distinctive periods and locations can potentially help identify the position of a transmitting civilization.

The project will investigate a simplified pulsar map inspired by the historical Pioneer plaques.

A final map must include verified pulsar identifiers, coordinates, reference epochs and timing information.

7.4 Important Limitation

A simple statement such as "Milky Way, Orion Spur" is not an exact astronomical address.

A useful positional reference requires a defined coordinate system, observation epoch and reliable astronomical measurements.

The final location package must therefore be prepared and independently checked before transmission.

9. Signal Propagation and Distance

Radio waves travel through vacuum at approximately the speed of light.

The signal can continue propagating after leaving Earth.

However, propagation distance and successful detection are different things.

The ability of another receiver to detect the message depends on:

- Transmitter power.
- Antenna gain.
- Transmission frequency.
- Signal bandwidth.
- Distance.
- Background radio noise.
- Receiver sensitivity.
- Integration time.
- Signal processing capability.

The project does not claim that the message will be detectable at any particular interstellar distance.

9.1 Propagation Time Examples

Destination| Approximate One-Way Light Time
Moon| 1.3 seconds
Mars| 3–22 minutes
Nearest star system| 4.2 years
Galactic Center| 26,000 years
Andromeda Galaxy| 2.5 million years

These examples illustrate the time required for electromagnetic signals to travel between astronomical locations.

16. Project Philosophy

A Voice from Earth is not an attempt to prove that someone is listening.

It is an attempt to preserve a human voice and send it beyond the boundaries of our familiar world.

The message may travel through space for thousands, millions or even billions of years.

It may never be detected.

It may never receive a response.

But its existence will represent a moment in human history when one individual decided to send a personal message into the unknown universe.
